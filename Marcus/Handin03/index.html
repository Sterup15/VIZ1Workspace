      How the score works and what it cannot tell us

      Count pairs, not matching members

      For each colour category, count the distinct pairs of members with that category. Divide their sum by all possible pairs with known colours.

      Matching score = matching pairs / all known-member pairs

      0%: every known category is different. 100%: all known categories match. Missing colours never count as matches. Fewer than two known colours means unavailable, not 0%.

      For example, Shai's bottoms have two black and two navy-blue records: 2 matching pairs out of 6 = 33.3%. This is not the percentage of members wearing one colour.

      Read the circle carefully

      The complete circle represents all 55 bands. Arc widths show each size group's share of those bands. Sizes run clockwise from 3 to 7, starting at the top; band size is not a cycle. The inner score guide is 0%, the middle 50%, and the outer 100%.

      Changing the garment preserves positions around the circle. Focusing a group keeps the other bands in view. An aligned dot plot would allow more accurate numerical comparisons than radial positions.

      Black and navy blue remain different categories. Two "patterned" entries match as categories, not necessarily as actual patterns. These records do not establish permanent band identities, styling intentions or causation.

      Source: the course-provided boyband dataset. 234 member records, not necessarily 234 distinct people.
