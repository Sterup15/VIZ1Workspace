What is being averaged, and how should I compare the wheels?

A band's score is its matching known-colour member pairs divided by all possible known-colour pairs. 0% means all known categories differ; 100% means they all match. For example, two black and two navy-blue records produce 2 matching pairs out of 6, or 33.3%.

Each centre value is the arithmetic mean of the available band scores in that size group. Every band has equal weight. It is not a pooled percentage of all member pairs, a percentage of matching members, or a percentage of completely matching bands.

Read the fraction filled within each band's own slot, not raw arc length across different wheels or tracks. More bands means narrower slots; the outer track has a larger radius. The centre numbers provide exact group comparisons. Band slots run alphabetically clockwise from the top, and the two garments always align.

Missing colours never match each other. Fewer than two known colours gives an unavailable score, shown as a hatched slot and excluded from the average, not treated as zero. A score based on incomplete colours is flagged. Black and navy blue are distinct categories; two "patterned" records need not depict identical patterns.

This alternative prioritizes group comparisons and simultaneous garment comparison. The original plot gives individual bands more prominent score positions. Neither layout establishes a causal band-size effect; an aligned dot plot would support more accurate numerical reading than curved marks.
